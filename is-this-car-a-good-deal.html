Check the numbers

  1. Find what it can sell for. Look at several similar cars listed near you, same model, close in year and miles. Use the middle price and take a little off. Asking prices are not selling prices.
  2. Add up the costs. Repairs, title, fees, a detail, tires. Count the small ones too.
  3. Set your profit first. Pick the number that makes the car worth your time, before you see the seller.
  4. Compare. Sale price minus costs minus profit is the most you should pay. If the asking price is higher, you have your reason to offer less.

Want just that last number? Use the max bid calculator.

Check the car

  • Title. Clean, and in the seller's name. No title, no deal.
  • Miles. High miles lower what it can sell for. Many listings leave them out, so ask.
  • Test drive. Warning lights, shifting, brakes, noises, leaks, rust and tires.
  • Negotiate from what you find. Every problem is a real cost. Offer less because of it, and walk if the price will not meet your number.

Questions

How do I know if a car is a good deal to flip?

Work out what the car can sell for from similar cars listed near you. Subtract your repairs, fees and the profit you want. If the asking price is at or below what is left, the numbers work. If it is above, offer less or walk away.

What should I check on the car itself?

Check the title is clean and in the seller's name, the miles, warning lights, rust, tires, fluid leaks and how it drives. Anything you find is a cost, and a reason to offer less.

Is the asking price what the car will sell for?

No. Asking prices are usually higher than selling prices. Use the middle of several similar listings and take a little off.

Is the deal check free?

Yes. It runs in your browser, needs no account, and the numbers you type are not sent anywhere.
