Car flipping costs: what to budget before you buy

The asking price is only one part of what a flip costs. Tax, title, repairs and selling costs all come out of the gap between what you pay and what the car can sell for. Here are the costs to plan for, so you can work them out before you make an offer.

All amounts are in US dollars. Every range on this page is a rough estimate from public price guides we checked in Nov 2026. Your area, your car and your shop will change them.

Fees differ by state. Each state sets its own sales tax, title fees, temporary permits and dealer license rules. Some counties add fees of their own. Check your state DMV before you budget.

1. The purchase

This is the biggest number, so set your limit before you see the car. Work out the most you should pay with the free max bid calculator. Then stick to it.

Buying at a public auction? Expect a buyer fee on top of the winning bid. Guides put it at roughly 5% to 17% of the bid. Some auctions also charge registration, online bidding or card fees (rough estimate). Read the auction's fee sheet first.

2. Sales tax

Most states charge sales tax, or a similar tax, when you title a used car. That includes cars bought from a private seller. A few states have no state sales tax. The tax usually goes to the state where you register the car, not the state where you bought it.

After the car itself, this is often the biggest cost. Example only: a $4,000 car at a 6% rate means $240 in tax.

3. Title and registration

Title transfer fees run from about $4 to about $215, depending on the state. Many states fall around $10 to $75 (rough estimate). Registration, plates and any required state inspection cost extra.

4. Getting it home

If the seller keeps the plates, you may need a temporary or trip permit. In states that offer one, it costs a few dollars up to about $15 (rough estimate). Some states do not offer one for private sales. You also need insurance to drive the car. If it does not run, get a tow quote before you agree on a price.

5. Inspection

A pre-purchase inspection at a shop costs about $100 to $300 (rough estimate). On cheaper cars you may do the check yourself. Our free Before You Buy checklist covers oil, coolant, tires, rust, dents and paint.

6. Repairs and parts

This is where most surprises hide. Rough shop estimates:

  • Oil change: about $35 to $125, depending on the oil type
  • Brake pads: about $130 to $400 per axle, and more if the rotors need replacing
  • Four tires, installed, on a common sedan: about $400 to $1,100
  • Big jobs, like a head gasket or a transmission, can cost more than the car is worth

Doing the work yourself saves on labor but costs you time. Price each repair before you make an offer. Then use what you found to explain a lower offer.

7. Cleaning and detailing

A full professional detail costs about $150 to $500 (rough estimate). Doing it yourself costs supplies and an afternoon. A clean car with good photos is easier to sell.

8. Selling costs

Some listing sites are free. Others charge, often for boosted ads. Add fuel and time for showing the car. Meet buyers somewhere safe, like a bank.

9. Holding costs

Each week the car sits costs you something. That means insurance, parking or storage, and cash you cannot use on the next car. If the car sits a long time, you may need to drop the price.

10. Dealer license limits

Many states limit how many cars a person can sell in a year without a dealer license. In the states we checked, the limit ran from about 2 to 6 cars a year. Going over it can bring fines. Check your state's rule before you start flipping often.

Add it up

Your profit on one car is what it sells for, minus all of the costs above. Put your own numbers into the free car flipping profit calculator to see what may be left. The result is an estimate, not a promise.

Questions

What is the biggest cost in a car flip, after the car itself?

Usually sales tax or repairs. The tax depends on your state and the price. Repairs depend on what the inspection finds. Price both before you make an offer.

Do I pay sales tax on a car I plan to resell?

In most states, yes, when you title the car in your name. Licensed dealers are often treated differently. The rules differ by state, so check with your state's tax office or DMV.

How much should I set aside for repairs?

There is no single number. Inspect the car, list what it needs, and get quotes or parts prices. Add a cushion for problems a test drive cannot show. If a car needs more than you can afford, walk away.

How many cars can I flip a year without a dealer license?

It depends on your state. In the states we checked, the limit was about 2 to 6 cars a year. Some states also look at whether you bought the car to resell it. Check with your state DMV or dealer board.
